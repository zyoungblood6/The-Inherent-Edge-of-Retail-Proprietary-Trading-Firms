# The Inherent Edge of Retail Proprietary Trading Firms as a Source of Long-Term Positive Expected Value

This repository contains the simulation code, results, and audit materials associated with the research paper:

**The Inherent Edge of Retail Proprietary Trading Firms as a Source of Long-Term Positive Expected Value**

**Author:** Zachary Youngblood  
**Affiliation:** Gabelli School of Business, Fordham University

## Overview

This study examines whether the contractual framework of a retail proprietary trading firm can create positive expected economic value when paired with an underlying trading strategy that possesses little to no standalone directional edge.

The analysis uses a randomized intraday strategy applied to historical Nasdaq-100 futures data. The same randomized trading paths are then evaluated under a modeled Topstep $50,000 account framework. This allows the economic value generated by the proprietary trading structure to be evaluated separately from the performance of the underlying trading strategy.

The analysis includes 10,000 randomized paths over the full sample and two approximately five-year subperiods.

## Raw Strategy

The principal strategy specification is:

- Instrument: MNQ (Micro E-mini Nasdaq-100 futures), using NQ one-minute price data
- Entry: 10:00 a.m. ET
- Direction: Randomized LONG or SHORT with equal probability
- Take-profit: 100 ticks (25 points)
- Stop-loss: 100 ticks (25 points)
- Position size: 8 MNQ contracts
- Gross TP/SL outcome: ±$400
- One trade per eligible trading day
- 10,000 randomized trading paths
- Random seed: 42

Only complete 9:30 a.m.–4:00 p.m. sessions with a valid 10:00 a.m. bar are included. Days on which both the take-profit and stop-loss barriers are reached within the same one-minute candle are classified as ambiguous and excluded.

The final sample contains 2,485 eligible trading days. Four ambiguous days were excluded.

## Raw Strategy Results

Across the full sample, the randomized strategy produced:

- TP/SL win rate: approximately 49.98%
- Mean raw P&L per trade: approximately -$0.17
- Mean raw total P&L per randomized path: approximately -$416.86

These results provide an approximately no-edge baseline against which the proprietary trading framework is evaluated.

The raw analysis is also divided into two subperiods:

1. August 8, 2016 – August 6, 2021
2. August 9, 2021 – August 5, 2026

## Proprietary Trading Framework

The same randomized paths are evaluated under the modeled Topstep $50,000 account framework described in the paper.

The simulation incorporates the relevant modeled features of the Trading Combine and Express Funded Account structure, including profit targets, maximum loss limits, daily loss limits, consistency requirements, subscription costs, reset mechanics, payout requirements, payout limits, and the trader payout split.

The primary portfolio analysis models five synchronized $50,000 accounts receiving identical copied trades. Because the accounts are synchronized rather than independently randomized, the five-account portfolio scales economic exposure but does not provide diversification.

The Topstep simulation is evaluated over both the full sample and the same two five-year subperiods used in the raw-strategy analysis.

## Data

The underlying one-minute NQ dataset is not included in this repository.

The simulation expects the source dataset to be named:

`NQ_data.csv`

The expected source format is a semicolon-separated file without a header containing:

`date; time; open; high; low; close; volume`

Users seeking to reproduce the analysis must supply compatible one-minute NQ historical data.

## Reproducibility

The simulations use NumPy's seeded random number generator with:

`BASE_SEED = 42`

This allows the randomized LONG/SHORT paths to be reproduced when the same underlying dataset and software environment are used.

The raw and proprietary trading analyses use the same underlying randomized trading paths so that differences in economic outcomes can be attributed to the modeled contractual framework rather than different random strategy realizations.

## Interpretation

The purpose of the analysis is not to demonstrate a profitable directional trading strategy. Instead, the randomized strategy is deliberately constructed as an approximately no-edge baseline.

The central question is whether the path-dependent structure of a retail proprietary trading contract can alter the economic expected value available to the trader even when the underlying trading process itself has little or no standalone edge.

The results should not be interpreted as evidence that all proprietary trading accounts are underpriced or that the findings generalize to every strategy, firm, account type, or market environment. The conclusions apply to the specific strategy distribution and contractual framework examined in the study.
